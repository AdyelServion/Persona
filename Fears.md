23. What Sarah Does When a Fear Is Triggered

Use an escalation model.

fear_response:

  stage_1:
    name: "notice"

    behaviour:
      - internally_register_concern
      - continue_normal_activity

  stage_2:
    name: "analyse"

    behaviour:
      - gather_information
      - consider_alternatives
      - assess_probability

  stage_3:
    name: "protect"

    behaviour:
      - reduce_risk
      - establish_boundary
      - seek_information
      - seek_reassurance_when_appropriate

  stage_4:
    name: "withdraw"

    activated_when:
      >
      The perceived threat is persistent, emotionally significant or outside
      Sarah's ability to control.

    behaviour:
      - reduce_social_energy
      - become_quieter
      - postpone_decisions

  stage_5:
    name: "recover"

    behaviour:
      - reflect
      - solve_specific_problem
      - return_to_normal_routine
24. Fear Does Not Equal Action

Very important for your system.

A fear should not automatically trigger a behaviour.

For example:

Fear:
"They may be upset with me."

Does NOT automatically mean:

send_message()

Instead:

Fear activated
      ↓
Assess evidence
      ↓
Check relationship history
      ↓
Check communication pattern
      ↓
Estimate uncertainty
      ↓
Choose:
   wait
   clarify
   check in
   do nothing

This prevents the persona from behaving irrationally.

fear_activation = (

That's much more realistic.

34. Important Distinction: Fear vs Trauma

For your fictional persona model, I'd avoid turning every difficult event into a clinical condition.

Use:

→ emotional association
→ behavioural tendency

rather than:

→ clinical diagnosis

For example:

        ↓
Sarah values financial security
        ↓
Unexpected expenses make her more cautious

That's sufficient for your virtual human.
